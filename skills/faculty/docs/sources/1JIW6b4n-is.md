## Contribution

An Oxford-inspired tutorial cycle starts with a question and prepared work, considers credible alternatives, defends a reasoned position and revises it after challenge. The useful mechanism is accountable preparation followed by adaptive feedback.

## Faculty application

- Define the question and expected artifact before collecting material.
- Check terminology and credible sources; distinguish evidence from opinion.
- Ask learners to explain their reasoning and address a defensible alternative.
- Make revision respond to the actual critique.
- Choose assessment appropriate to the discipline: proof, performance or explanation as needed.

## Evidence boundaries

Tutorial frequency, essay length and assessment vary by course and college. Steelmanning is the creator's adaptation, not a universal Oxford requirement. Oral defense cannot replace mathematical proof or working software; read-aloud practice is optional. Do not manufacture false balance where evidence is asymmetric. Seven-day or semester-equivalence promises were not established.
