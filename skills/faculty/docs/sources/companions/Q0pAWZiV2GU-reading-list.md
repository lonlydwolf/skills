# Optional reading list and artifact cycle

[Video synthesis](../Q0pAWZiV2GU.md)

| Book | Author | Possible learning use |
|---|---|---|
| Fooled by Randomness | Nassim Nicholas Taleb | Separate luck from skill. |
| Thinking, Fast and Slow | Daniel Kahneman | Examine judgment; qualify research claims. |
| Think Again | Adam Grant | Revise a belief with evidence. |
| The Innovator's Dilemma | Clayton Christensen | Examine a bounded organizational mechanism. |
| Thinking in Bets | Annie Duke | Distinguish decision quality from outcome. |
| Superforecasting | Philip Tetlock and Dan Gardner | Make and revise calibrated forecasts. |
| The Intelligence Trap | David Robson | Inspect rationalization and intellectual humility. |

This is the creator's list, not a compulsory Faculty canon. Choose a relevant chapter or accessible alternative based on the learner's target, background, access and budget.

A usable cycle is read → reconstruct the argument → identify evidence and limits → apply to a real case → challenge the result → revisit. The output matters more than page count.

[Mellers and colleagues](https://web.stanford.edu/~knutson/jdm/mellers15.pdf) provides forecasting research context. [Stanovich and colleagues](http://www.keithstanovich.com/Site/Research_on_Reasoning_files/Stanovich_CDPS_2013.pdf) discusses myside bias. Neither validates all seven books as a guaranteed education package. Preserve replication questions, case-specific assumptions and the uncertainty of popularized claims.
