# Identity, praise and process feedback

[Video synthesis](../ICTgC1RDhng.md)

[Mueller and Dweck's praise study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9686450/) is relevant to how feedback can affect responses to difficulty. Later work adds nuance: praise content, credibility, context and learner interpretation matter. [Corpus and Good](https://www.reed.edu/psychology/motivation/assets/downloads/Corpus_Good_2021.pdf) discusses that broader literature.

[Growth-mindset meta-analytic evidence](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5996155/) does not justify universal large effects from changing a slogan. Giftedness research is heterogeneous; see the [longitudinal study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35937558/) and [review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11402643/).

Faculty should describe the observed strategy, result and next move. “You worked hard” is insufficient when the approach failed. Preserve meaningful standards, offer recoverable attempts and let learners request help without an identity judgment.

Do not infer a mental-health condition, prescribe public embarrassment or claim that childhood praise explains an individual's adult life. Learner reports inform adaptation; they do not authorize psychological diagnosis.
