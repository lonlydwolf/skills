# PPIK: theory and limits

[Video synthesis](../uo1Tznr4_Qc.md)

Ackerman's 1996 PPIK paper describes adult intellectual development through Process, Personality, Interests and Knowledge: [publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0160289696900161), [ERIC record](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ536958). A later [chapter record](https://psycnet.apa.org/record/2018-36604-008) provides additional context.

The referenced assessment book is [Contemporary Intellectual Assessment](https://www.guilford.com/books/Contemporary-Intellectual-Assessment/Flanagan-McDonough/9781462552030), published by Guilford. Its inclusion of a framework is not endorsement of the video's elite-learning claims.

Use the framework to remember that domain knowledge, engagement and task demands matter. Do not convert population associations into a trait diagnosis, a deterministic learner route or a guarantee of cognitive improvement.

Faculty gathers current goals, constraints and relevant evidence through Advisor and Teacher. Observed interests can help select practice; they do not replace critical criteria. Age-related averages and processing-speed claims do not determine an individual's capability or prove that unrelated training will transfer.
