## Contribution

The source introduces Ackerman's PPIK framework: Process, Personality, Interests and Knowledge. Its educational contribution is attending to both task demands and sustained domain engagement rather than reducing learning to one general ability score.

## Faculty application

- Ask about learner goals, constraints, relevant prior knowledge and observed interests.
- Offer useful routes into sustained practice and domain knowledge.
- Base adaptations on current work and learner reports, not inferred trait labels.
- Keep changes provisional when evidence is incomplete.

## Evidence boundaries

PPIK is a descriptive theory of adult intellectual development, not a validated elite-learning recipe. The referenced textbook is a Guilford publication, not an exclusive Harvard formula. Age-related averages, speed claims and trainability claims do not justify diagnosing an individual. Correlations between interests, traits and knowledge do not establish a guaranteed intervention. Paid coaching context should remain visible.
