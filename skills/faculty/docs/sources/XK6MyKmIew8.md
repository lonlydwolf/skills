# Elon Musk's 3 Rules To Learning Anything

Creator: Better Chapter compilation featuring Elon Musk · Duration: 3:19 · Published: 2021-09-06

[Original video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XK6MyKmIew8)

## Contribution

The compilation recommends reasoning from premises, checking logical steps and considering probabilities. The useful move is distinguishing an accepted assumption from a conclusion and asking what evidence would change the result.

## Faculty application

- State premises, derivation and uncertainty separately.
- Verify foundational claims through suitable sources.
- Use first-principles reasoning when the learner has enough domain knowledge.
- Use analogies with structural checks and explicit limits rather than treating analogy as inherently inferior.

## Evidence boundaries

First-principles reasoning is not Musk's invention. Historical battery and rocket cost examples are not current prices or complete manufacturing analyses. The opening Socrates quotation was not established. Novices still need trustworthy models and foundations; asking them to derive everything unaided can overload learning. Probability language should not manufacture precision without evidence.
