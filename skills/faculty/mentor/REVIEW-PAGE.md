# The review report

One page per review. It has no navigation: there is nothing above it to go up to,
and it is reached only by being named when the review closes. Its reader is the
junior whose work was reviewed, reading it alone with nobody to ask.

The page title is `Review — {YYYY-MM-DD}`, and the date is on the page as well as
in its name: the filename is not where a reader looks.

The test is sufficiency:

> Everything a senior judges the junior needs in order to grow. Where you are
> unsure, include it.

Reading alone is what sets the length. There is no exchange to fall back on, so
a finding that assumes wrong is a finding lost.

## Scope

What this review covered and where it stopped — the branch, the commit range, or
the named artifacts. It is the same boundary the log line records, stated here so
the page is readable on its own.

## Findings

Each carries its severity **as a word**, then the problem, why it matters, where
it is, and the background it turns on. What that background owes and where it
stops is in [SKILL.md](SKILL.md#review-voice); it binds every medium rather than
only this page.

## The retro

What went well, what dragged, what to change before next time — the outcomes as
they were agreed, in the junior's hands rather than only in the machine's file.

## The build or project as a whole

What the senior sees about the shape of the thing being built, beyond the scope
reviewed — observation only. It never proposes a scope change, a new milestone,
or a reordering. Where an observation implies one, say so and name the route: in
a course that is `/academic-advisor`.
